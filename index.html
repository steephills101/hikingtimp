Mt. Timpanogos

A practical guide to planning one of Utah's most iconic high-elevation mountain hikes.

Start Planning Safety First

Know Before You Go

Mt. Timpanogos is a serious mountain hike. Conditions can change rapidly with elevation, so plan for a long day and be prepared for changing weather.

Summit Elevation 11,749 ft
Mountain Range Wasatch Range
Location Utah County, Utah
Difficulty Strenuous

⚠️ Important

Trail distances, access, snow conditions, parking rules, and seasonal hazards can change. Check current conditions and official land-management information before starting your hike.

Popular Routes

Two commonly discussed approaches to Mt. Timpanogos are the Timpooneke Trail and the Aspen Grove route.

Timpooneke Trail

Strenuous High Elevation Long Day

A popular approach from the American Fork Canyon side. The trail climbs through forest and alpine terrain before reaching the upper mountain and summit area.

Aspen Grove Route

Strenuous Steep Alpine Terrain

The Aspen Grove approach is another well-known way to reach Mt. Timpanogos. Expect substantial elevation gain and demanding terrain.

Mountain Safety

Timpanogos is beautiful, but the elevation and exposed terrain make preparation extremely important.

🌦️

Watch the Weather

Mountain weather can change quickly. Check the forecast before leaving and be prepared to turn around.

⛰️

Respect Elevation

The summit is nearly 12,000 feet above sea level. Pace yourself, stay hydrated, and recognize symptoms of altitude problems.

⚡

Lightning

Avoid exposed ridges and summits during thunderstorms. Start early enough to reduce afternoon storm exposure.

❄️

Snow & Ice

Snow can remain at high elevations well after lower trails become dry. Conditions may require specialized equipment and experience.

🧭

Navigation

Carry an offline map and know your route. Do not depend entirely on cell service.

🐐

Wildlife

Keep your distance from wildlife and never feed animals. Mountain goats and other wildlife are best observed from afar.

Seasonal Considerations

Conditions on the mountain can be dramatically different from conditions in nearby cities and valleys.

Season What to Expect Planning Consideration
Spring Snow can persist at high elevations. Check snowpack and route conditions carefully.
Summer Generally the main hiking season. Start early and watch afternoon weather.
Fall Cooler temperatures and shorter daylight. Carry warm layers and plan your turnaround time.
Winter Snow, ice, and avalanche hazards may exist. Requires appropriate winter skills and equipment.
